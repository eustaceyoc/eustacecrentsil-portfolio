A bold, red-and-black website for Eckert Electric

A small, local father-and-son electrical business in East Cobb, Marietta, Georgia, run by Randy Eckert. It had no website, so I built a sample site around what neighbours say about it.

01 / The business

25 years in East Cobb

Eckert Electric is a small electrical business in East Cobb, Marietta, Georgia. On Nextdoor, Randy Eckert says he has been servicing East Cobb for 25 years, helping the area "with any and all electrical needs", and gives his direct number. Neighbours describe it as a father-and-son business, naming Randy and Noah.

It was a Nextdoor Neighborhood Fave every year from 2021 to 2026, and 162 neighbours have faved it. Its listed hours are Monday to Saturday, 8 am to 5 pm.

Sources: the business's Nextdoor page, checked October 2026.

02 / The problem

Six years of awards, all on one app

Six Neighborhood Fave awards in a row and 162 faves is a lot of local trust, but it lives on Nextdoor. Someone who isn't on the app, or who searches for an electrician, won't see it. I wanted a site that lets neighbours' words do the selling and puts Randy's direct line front and centre.

03 / Approach

Three words, in neighbours' own voices

Neighbours keep using the same words: quick, reasonable, reliable. So those words became the headline, and the approach section is a set of expandable rows, one for each quality, each opening to what a neighbour wrote. The look is strong and simple: black, white and a single signal red, with big numbers.

What changed between versions

The first version had a section called "How Randy works, in his words" and another called "The numbers, from public pages". I rebuilt the page around neighbours' words instead: the headline became the three words they keep using, and the approach section became expandable rows, each one opening to a neighbour's review.

Palette

One signal red, used sparingly for accents and the call button, with black, white and a warm paper colour. Greys handle labels and rules.

Type

The whole site is set in one family, Inter Tight, a narrower cut of Inter. Headlines use a very heavy weight, and body text the regular weight.

Layout

  • Hero. The headline, call and "Ask Randy" buttons, and a fact panel: 25 years, area, Nextdoor awards and the direct line.
  • Approach. "How neighbors describe the work." Five expandable rows, each opening to a neighbour's words.
  • Facts. "The record, from Nextdoor." A dark band with three big numbers: 25 years, 6× Neighborhood Fave and 162 faves.
  • Neighbours. A carousel of full Nextdoor quotes with initials and dates, with a pause button.
  • Contact. "Ask Randy." The direct number, his own words, the weekly hours and a short form.

Interactions

The approach rows open and close, and the quotes carousel moves on its own but can be paused. The form checks each field. With reduced motion turned on, the animations are switched off.

Mobile

Below 900 px the hero and grids stack, and below 520 px the type and spacing tighten. A "Call Randy" bar stays fixed to the bottom of the screen.

04 / Build

One small file, hosted on Vercel

The site is a single static HTML file with its CSS and a little plain JavaScript inside it. No framework and no build step. It's hosted on Vercel.

  • Light. The HTML file is about 33 KB, and it uses one font family from Google Fonts.
  • Accessible. Real headings and landmarks, rows and carousel controls that work with a keyboard, labelled form fields, and the reduced-motion setting respected.

Safeguards

Because this is a sample site for a real business that didn't ask for it, I put a few things in place:

  • A banner at the top and a line in the footer say it is a sample site, not affiliated with or published by the business.
  • It's hidden from search engines with a noindex meta tag.
  • The form is a demo. It sends nothing and says so.
  • Every fact comes from the business's public listings, and the page says where each one came from.
  • No photos or logos are used.
