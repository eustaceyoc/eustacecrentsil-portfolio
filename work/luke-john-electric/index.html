01 / The business

A family business, licensed and insured

Luke & John Electric is a family electrical business in Atlanta, Georgia. Its Nextdoor page invites people to call it to "repair, install, and upgrade anything electrical in your home", describes it as licensed and insured, and lists its licence number, EN217337. Neighbours mention lighting, outlets, recessed lighting and panel replacement.

It was a Nextdoor Neighborhood Fave every year from 2021 to 2025, with 218 faves, and has a 4.9 rating on Google from 67 reviews.

Sources: the business's Nextdoor page and Google listing, checked October 2026.

02 / The problem

Higher-end work, everyday listings

Neighbours describe careful, thorough work, like panel replacements with the circuits labelled afterwards. But it's all on listing sites, next to every other electrician. I wanted a site that feels as careful as the work. The public listings show different phone numbers and hours, so I left those off and pointed people to email instead.

03 / Approach

Like a well-made home

Ivory, deep navy and brass, the colours of good fixtures, with an elegant serif and plenty of space. Sections are numbered in Roman numerals (I to IV), the services are "Two things done well." (lighting and panel upgrades), and the hero has a small light-switch toggle that turns the page's light on.

Palette

A warm ivory for the page, a deep navy for type and dark sections, and brass for accents and rules, with a lighter brass for highlights on navy.

Type

Headlines are set in Cormorant Garamond, a refined, high-contrast serif, often in italics. Body text and labels are in Manrope, a modern sans.

Display · Cormorant Garamond

Residential electrical, done with care.

Text · Manrope

A family electrical business. Call us to repair, install, and upgrade anything electrical in your home.

Layout

  • Hero. The headline, a consultation button and a light-switch toggle, next to facts: family-run, Atlanta, Neighborhood Fave 2021 to 2025.
  • Services. "Two things done well." Tabs for lighting, panel upgrades and anything else electrical, each with jobs and where they were mentioned.
  • Licence. A navy band: licensed and insured, with the licence number from the Nextdoor page.
  • Neighbours' words. A three-slide quote carousel, then the faves, awards and Google rating.
  • Consultation. "Tell us about your home." An email form, with the email address for anyone who'd rather write directly.

Interactions

The light switch in the hero turns on a warm glow, the brass rules draw themselves in as you scroll, and the services work as tabs. The quotes carousel has arrow buttons and a counter, and the form checks each field. With reduced motion turned on, the animations are switched off.

Mobile

Below 900 px the hero and grids stack, and below 520 px the type and spacing tighten. A consultation button stays fixed to the bottom of the screen.

04 / Build

One small file, hosted on Vercel

The site is a single static HTML file with its CSS and a little plain JavaScript inside it. No framework and no build step. It's hosted on Vercel.

  • Light. The HTML file is about 30 KB, and it uses two font families from Google Fonts.
  • Accessible. Real headings and landmarks, tabs and carousel controls that work with a keyboard, labelled form fields, and the reduced-motion setting respected.

Safeguards

Because this is a sample site for a real business that didn't ask for it, I put a few things in place:

  • A banner at the top and a line in the footer say it is a sample site, not affiliated with or published by the business.
  • It's hidden from search engines with a noindex meta tag.
  • The form is a demo. It sends nothing and says so.
  • Every fact comes from the business's public listings, and the page says where each one came from.
  • No photos or logos are used.
