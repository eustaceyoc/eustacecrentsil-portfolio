A job-list website for Devcraft Handyman Services

01 / The business

Minor fixes to larger renovations

Devcraft Handyman Services, in Centennial, Colorado, describes itself as a seasoned general handyman with over a decade of experience, for "minor fixes to larger renovations". On Nextdoor it's listed for handyman work, drywall repair, flooring, house painting and plumbing, and its posts mention hourly or flat-rate pricing. It works Monday to Saturday, 10 am to 5 pm.

It has 46 faves on Nextdoor, where it was a Neighborhood Fave in 2022, 2024 and 2025. Neighbours name Devin in their reviews.

Sources: the business's Nextdoor page, checked October 2026.

02 / The problem

"Do you do that?"

The hardest part of hiring a handyman is knowing whether they do your particular job. Devcraft's reviews answer that well (plumbing, lights, a lock, a wall patch), but they're buried on Nextdoor. I wanted the site to lead with the actual job list and make clear that small jobs are welcome.

03 / Approach

A job board, not a brochure

The layout works like a well-organized workshop board. A fixed side panel on the left holds the name, numbered sections, phone number and hours. The main column leads with "What gets fixed.": category chips you can tap to highlight matching jobs, and job cards taken from neighbours' reviews. There are no photos.

What changed between versions

In a second pass I added the "Small jobs welcome" section, made the category chips work (tap one to highlight matching jobs), turned the reviews into a carousel and added form validation. The page grew from about 13 KB to about 27 KB.

Palette

A slate blue and deep navy for the side panel and dark sections, a pine green for accents and selected chips, with a light mint, on a warm white.

Headlines and labels are set in Archivo, a sturdy sans, at a heavy weight. Quotes and some body text are in Source Serif 4, often in italics, which gives neighbours' words a warmer voice.

Layout

  • Side panel. Fixed on the left on desktop: the name, four numbered sections, the phone number, hours and a quote button.
  • The job list. Category chips (All, Handyman, Drywall repair, Flooring, House painting, Plumbing) and four job cards from neighbours' reviews.
  • Small jobs welcome. A dark section: "Large and small projects.", the pricing note and a neighbour's quote.
  • Neighbours. "Prompt, and on time." A review carousel, with the faves and awards.
  • Quote. A request form with an optional photo, which stays on the device in this demo.

Interactions

Tapping a category chip highlights the matching job cards, and the chips report their state to screen readers. The reviews sit in a carousel with arrow buttons, and the form checks each field before "sending". With reduced motion turned on, the animations are switched off.

Mobile

Below 900 px the side panel turns into a header at the top of the page, and below 520 px the type and spacing tighten. A call button stays fixed to the bottom of the screen.

04 / Build

One small file, hosted on Vercel

The site is a single static HTML file with its CSS and a little plain JavaScript inside it. No framework and no build step. It's hosted on Vercel.

  • Light. The HTML file is about 27 KB, and it uses two font families from Google Fonts.
  • Accessible. Real headings and landmarks, chips that announce whether they're selected, labelled form fields with clear errors, and the reduced-motion setting respected.

Safeguards

Because this is a sample site for a real business that didn't ask for it, I put a few things in place:

  • A banner at the top and a line in the footer say it is a sample site, not affiliated with or published by the business.
  • It's hidden from search engines with a noindex meta tag.
  • The form is a demo. It sends nothing and says so.
  • Every fact comes from the business's public listings, and the page says where each one came from.
  • No photos or logos are used.
