01 / The business

A young business with checkable credentials

Jaylee Electric LLC is a local electrical contractor in Fort Worth, Texas, owned by Gilbert LaQue III. According to its BBB profile it has been in business since October 2023, and it has been BBB accredited, with an A+ rating, since April 2024. It lists electrical wiring, panel upgrades and commercial electrical work, and describes itself as fully insured and licensed.

Neighbours on Nextdoor named it a Neighborhood Favorite in 2025. It works weekdays from 8 am.

Sources: the business's public Nextdoor page, BBB profile and BuildZoom contractor licence records, checked October 2026.

02 / The problem

A new business has to earn trust faster

A business that started in 2023 doesn't have decades of reviews to point to. What it does have is checkable: a BBB A+ rating, a licence and a Nextdoor award. But without a website, someone looking for an electrician has to find those things on their own.

I wanted the site to put those credentials up front, each one linked to where you can check it.

03 / Approach

Black, yellow and straight to the point

Black and a bright safety yellow feel like the trade, and the copy stays short and direct, using the owner's own line, "No job too big or small." Instead of a reviews section, there's a "Credentials you can check" section, because there are only a few public reviews to quote so far.

A shared trade-site structure

Before building these sites I did some design research on local trade websites and noted what the good ones have in common: a full-bleed hero, a strip of trust signals, a simple three-step "how it works", a band with a big phone number, and gentle motion. Four of the sample sites (Exceptional Lawn Care, Clear Water Pool Service, Jaylee Electric and Alloyd Garage Door) share that structure. The palette, photos and copy are specific to each business.

Palette

A near-black for the hero and dark sections, a bright safety yellow for buttons and highlights, and a dark gold for accents on light backgrounds, where bright yellow wouldn't read.

Type

Headlines are set in Bricolage Grotesque at a heavy weight, which feels sturdy and a little hand-made. Body text is Inter, a plain sans that reads well at small sizes on phones.

Layout

  • Hero. A photo of electrical equipment, the headline, call and request buttons, and a trust strip: the 2025 award, the BBB rating, and licensed and insured.
  • Services. Three cards for the service categories on the BBB profile, with a note that the full list would be confirmed with the owner.
  • How it works. Three steps: give us a shout, tell us about the job, professional work.
  • Credentials. A grid of facts, each with its source: the business's own Nextdoor post, a neighbour's recommendation, the BBB rating, the LLC details and the 2025 award.
  • Contact. A dark band with the phone number, then hours, service area and a request form.

Interactions

Sections fade up gently as they come into view, and the hero photo zooms very slowly. If your device is set to reduce motion, both are switched off.

Mobile

Below 760 px it's one column, with a bar fixed to the bottom of the screen for "Call now" and "Request".

04 / Build

One small file, hosted on Vercel

The site is a single static HTML file with its CSS and a little plain JavaScript inside it. No framework and no build step. It's hosted on Vercel.

  • Light. The HTML file is about 33 KB, and it uses two font families from Google Fonts.
  • Accessible. A skip link, real headings and landmarks, labels on every form field, and the reduced-motion setting respected.

Safeguards

Because this is a sample site for a real business that didn't ask for it, I put a few things in place:

  • A banner at the top and a line in the footer say it is a sample site, not affiliated with or published by the business.
  • It's hidden from search engines with a noindex meta tag.
  • The form is a demo. It sends nothing and says so.
  • Every fact comes from the business's public listings, and the page says where each one came from.
  • Photos are from Unsplash, credited in the footer. They are stock images, not the business's own work.
