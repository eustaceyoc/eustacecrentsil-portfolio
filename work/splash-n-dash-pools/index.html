01 / The business

Pool care, all year round

Splash n Dash Pools is listed on Nextdoor as a pool cleaner and pool repair service in Sacramento, California, and on Google as a pool cleaning service. In its own words it offers "the best service for the best price in your city" and pool maintenance "all year round".

It has 10 faves on Nextdoor, where it was a Neighborhood Fave in 2024 and 2025. In a reply to a Google review, the business names Sal as the person who did the work.

Sources: the business's Nextdoor page and Google listing, checked October 2026.

02 / The problem

Little to go on, so keep it honest

The business doesn't publish prices or a detailed list of services, and there are only a few reviews. I didn't want to fill the gaps with made-up claims. So the site says what's listed, says plainly that pricing and scope are a phone call, and makes that call easy.

03 / Approach

Keep the services easy to read

I chose warm stone and lagoon colours rather than bright pool blue. Each service names its source, and the page explains that the business doesn't publish prices. Visitors can call to discuss the work.

Palette

Warm stone backgrounds, a deep lagoon for dark sections and buttons, a mid lagoon for accents and a pale aqua for highlights. Text is a dark green-black.

Type

Headlines are set in Instrument Serif, a narrow, elegant serif, with italics for emphasis. Body text is Figtree, a friendly geometric sans.

Text · Figtree

Cleaning, repair and maintenance for Sacramento pools, from a local business neighbors name a Neighborhood Fave.

Layout

  • Hero. The headline, the phone number as the main button, and a photo panel with the three services.
  • Services. "Three things, done simply." Rows that open for cleaning, repair and year-round maintenance, each with its source and a note that pricing isn't published.
  • Why neighbours call. The faves and the two Neighborhood Fave years, next to the business's own line from Nextdoor.
  • Good words. A small two-slide carousel: a Google review excerpt and the business's reply.
  • Contact. The phone number in large type, the email, and a request form that checks each field.

Interactions

Sections fade in gently as you scroll, the service rows open and close, the review carousel has arrow buttons and a counter, and the form checks each field and explains what's missing. With reduced motion turned on, the animations are switched off.

Mobile

Below 960 px the hero and sections stack, and below 480 px the type and spacing tighten for small phones. A call button stays fixed to the bottom of the screen.

04 / Build

One small file, hosted on Vercel

I built this as one HTML file with CSS and a little JavaScript. There's no framework or build step. It runs on Vercel.

  • The build is small. The HTML file is about 28 KB, and it uses two font families from Google Fonts.
  • I used real headings and landmarks, labelled form fields with clear error messages, and the reduced-motion setting respected.

Safeguards

I made this as a sample, so I added these safeguards.

  • A banner at the top and a line in the footer say it is a sample site, not affiliated with or published by the business.
  • It's hidden from search engines with a noindex meta tag.
  • The form is a demo. It sends nothing and says so.
  • Every fact comes from the business's public listings, and the page says where each one came from.
  • Photos are from Unsplash, credited in the footer and labelled as stock on the page.
