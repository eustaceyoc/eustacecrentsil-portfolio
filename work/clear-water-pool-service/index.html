01 / The business

Two owners, one pool at a time

Clear Water Pool Service has been family owned and operated in Arlington, Texas, by Jamie and Jimmy Gallagher since 2010. They do regular pool service (netting, brushing, emptying baskets), filter and chemical care, and bigger jobs like repairs, draining, acid washes and power washing.

Neighbours named it a Nextdoor Neighborhood Favorite in 2023, and it averages 4.9 on Angi from 59 reviews.

Sources: the business's public Nextdoor page and Angi listing, checked October 2026.

02 / The problem

Good reviews, spread across listing sites

Years in business and a 4.9 rating say a lot, but I couldn't find a website. Someone thinking about a pool service has to piece together what's included and how pricing works from different listings.

I wanted one page that explains what happens on a visit, how pricing works and how to ask for a free estimate.

03 / Approach

Calm, cool and clear about price

Pool owners want two things: clean water and no surprises. So the page uses deep navy and bright aqua, like looking into a clean pool, and gives pricing its own section instead of hiding it.

A shared trade-site structure

Before building these sites I did some design research on local trade websites and noted what the good ones have in common: a full-bleed hero, a strip of trust signals, a simple three-step "how it works", a band with a big phone number, and gentle motion. Four of the sample sites (Exceptional Lawn Care, Clear Water Pool Service, Jaylee Electric and Alloyd Garage Door) share that structure. The palette, photos and copy are specific to each business.

Palette

A deep navy for the hero and dark sections, a bright aqua for buttons and highlights, and a darker teal for accents on light backgrounds. The page background is a pale, cool blue-white.

Type

Headlines are set in Bricolage Grotesque at a heavy weight, which feels sturdy and a little hand-made. Body text is Inter, a plain sans that reads well at small sizes on phones.

Display · Bricolage Grotesque

We do the cleaning. You do the swimming.

Text · Inter

Net the surface, brush the entire pool and steps, empty the skimmer and pump baskets.

Layout

  • Hero. A dark photo of pool water behind the headline, two buttons, and a trust strip: the 2023 award, family-owned since 2010, and the Angi rating.
  • Services. Three cards (pool service, filters and chemicals, repairs and bigger jobs), each with what's included.
  • How it works. Three steps, ending with "Go for a swim". The estimate step explains that price depends on the pool's size and the trees around it, with no extra charges without discussing them first.
  • Reviews. Excerpts from Angi reviews with names and dates, plus a tile for the year the business started.
  • Pricing and contact. "Clear pricing, no surprises." next to what's included, the service area, a short story and the estimate form.

Interactions

Sections fade up gently as they come into view, and the hero photo zooms very slowly. If your device is set to reduce motion, both are switched off.

Mobile

Below 760 px everything stacks into one column, and a bar fixed to the bottom of the screen keeps "Call now" and "Estimate" one tap away.

04 / Build

One small file, hosted on Vercel

The site is a single static HTML file with its CSS and a little plain JavaScript inside it. No framework and no build step. It's hosted on Vercel.

  • Light. The HTML file is about 34 KB, and it uses two font families from Google Fonts.
  • Accessible. A skip link, real headings and landmarks, labels on every form field, and the reduced-motion setting respected.

Safeguards

Because this is a sample site for a real business that didn't ask for it, I put a few things in place:

  • A banner at the top and a line in the footer say it is a sample site, not affiliated with or published by the business.
  • It's hidden from search engines with a noindex meta tag.
  • The form is a demo. It sends nothing and says so.
  • Every fact comes from the business's public listings, and the page says where each one came from.
  • Photos are from Unsplash, credited in the footer. They are stock images, not the Gallaghers' own work.
