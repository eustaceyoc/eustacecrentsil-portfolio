01 / The business

Safe-to-code, and quick to reply

Dreher Electrical, in Peoria, Arizona, offers what it calls quality, safe-to-code electrical services at an affordable price. Its Nextdoor page lists lighting, dedicated circuits, fans, remodels and troubleshooting, and its own posts show EV charger installs. Neighbours name Michael in their reviews.

It has a 5.0 rating on Google from 44 reviews and 74 faves on Nextdoor, where it was a Neighborhood Fave in 2022 and 2026. It can be booked online through Google, and its own advice to a neighbour was simply, "Shoot me a text."

Sources: the business's Nextdoor page and Google listing, checked October 2026.

02 / The problem

Great reviews, no website

Forty-four five-star Google reviews is a strong record, but without a website the details are scattered across Google and Nextdoor. I wanted one page that shows what Dreher does, what neighbours say, and the quickest way to reach them, which is a text.

03 / Approach

Use the tools of the trade

I designed the page around an electrical meter readout instead of photos. A faint grid sits behind a business-details panel. Service labels run from SVC-01 to SVC-06, and reviews appear as field reports.

Palette

A near-black blue-grey for the page, a bright yellow for buttons and highlights, and a cyan for live-status details like the blinking dot. Text is a soft off-white, with grey for labels.

Layout

  • Hero. The headline and a text button, next to the "Business readout" panel: location, Google rating, Nextdoor faves and awards, booking, hours and contact.
  • Capabilities. Six numbered service cards, each with evidence: a line from a review or one of the business's own posts.
  • Sequence. "One job, start to finish." Five steps from research to done, summarized from one neighbour's Nextdoor review.
  • Field reports. Neighbours' reviews with initials and dates, and chips for the topics Google lists from its reviews, with counts.
  • Contact. "Got a job? Text us." with the number in large type and a request form.

Interactions

A status dot in the readout blinks, and a couple of small animations give the page a live, powered-on feel. On phones, "Text us" and "Call" buttons stay fixed to the bottom of the screen.

Mobile

Below 960 px the readout moves under the headline and the grids drop to two columns, and below 600 px everything stacks in one column.

04 / Build

One small file, hosted on Vercel

I built this as one HTML file with CSS and a little JavaScript. There's no framework or build step. It runs on Vercel.

  • The build is small. The HTML file is about 20 KB, and it uses two font families from Google Fonts.
  • I used headings, page landmarks and labelled form fields.

Safeguards

I made this as a sample, so I added these safeguards.

  • A banner at the top and a line in the footer say it is a sample site, not affiliated with or published by the business.
  • It's hidden from search engines with a noindex meta tag.
  • The form is a demo. It sends nothing and says so.
  • Every fact comes from the business's public listings, and the page says where each one came from.
  • No photos or logos are used.
