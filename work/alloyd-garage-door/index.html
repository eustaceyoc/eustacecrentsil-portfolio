01 / The business

Wade, the neighbourhood's garage door guy

Alloyd Garage Door & Opener Repair is an owner-operated business in Duncanville, Texas. It fixes broken springs and cables, repairs and replaces openers, and installs new doors, with free estimates. It's open weekdays from 8 am to 4 pm and accepts credit cards.

It has been BBB accredited since 1999 with an A+ rating, averages 4.9 on Angi from 7 reviews, and was a Nextdoor Neighborhood Favorite every year from 2021 to 2025. One Angi reviewer says Wade has been their garage door guy for over 20 years.

Sources: the business's BBB profile, Angi listing and Nextdoor page, checked October 2026.

02 / The problem

Decades of trust, hard to find

When a spring snaps, people need a phone number fast. Alloyd has a long record (BBB accredited since 1999, five Nextdoor awards in a row), but that record is spread across three listing sites. I wanted one page that answers "can you fix it, and how do I reach you?" straight away.

03 / Approach

Lead with the broken door

I led with the question a customer is asking, 'Garage door stuck? Call Alloyd.' Orange call buttons stand out against the navy background. The text explains that Wade runs the business and offers free estimates.

A shared trade-site structure

I researched local trade websites before building these samples. Exceptional Lawn Care, Clear Water Pool Service, Jaylee Electric and Alloyd Garage Door share a layout with a large hero photo, reviews or credentials, a short process section and a prominent phone number. I changed the colours, photos and copy for each business.

What changed between versions

The first version led with the business's name, set in system fonts, and had a "Why homeowners choose Alloyd" section. I rebuilt it on the shared trade-site structure: the headline became the customer's problem ("Garage door stuck? Call Alloyd."), the type moved to Bricolage Grotesque and Inter, and the reasons-to-choose section became three plain steps: call, free estimate, fixed.

Palette

A deep navy for the hero and dark sections, a strong safety orange for buttons, and an amber for small highlights, on a warm off-white.

I used bold Bricolage Grotesque for headings and Inter for body text. Inter stays readable at small sizes on phones.

Text · Inter

Broken springs and cables, opener repair and new installs for homes in Duncanville and the surrounding area.

Layout

  • Hero. A garage photo behind the headline, call and estimate buttons, the hours, and a trust strip: BBB A+ since 1999, 4.9 on Angi and five Nextdoor awards.
  • Services. Three cards: garage door repair, new doors and replacement, openers.
  • How it works. Three steps: call, free estimate, fixed.
  • Reviews. "Neighbors keep calling Wade." Excerpts from Angi reviews with names and dates, and a 5× Nextdoor tile.
  • Hours and estimate. Opening hours as listed on Nextdoor, the service area, and an estimate form that asks what's wrong (spring, door, opener, new door).

Interactions

Sections fade up gently as they come into view, and the hero photo zooms very slowly. If your device is set to reduce motion, both are switched off.

Mobile

Below 760 px it's one column, with a bar fixed to the bottom of the screen for calling and the estimate form.

04 / Build

One small file, hosted on Vercel

I built this as one HTML file with CSS and a little JavaScript. There's no framework or build step. It runs on Vercel.

  • The build is small. The HTML file is about 32 KB, and it uses two font families from Google Fonts.
  • I added a skip link, headings, page landmarks and labelled form fields. Animations respect reduced-motion settings.

Safeguards

I made this as a sample, so I added these safeguards.

  • A banner at the top and a line in the footer say it is a sample site, not affiliated with or published by the business.
  • It's hidden from search engines with a noindex meta tag.
  • The form is a demo. It sends nothing and says so.
  • Every fact comes from the business's public listings, and the page says where each one came from.
  • Photos are from Unsplash, credited in the footer. They are stock images, not of the business.
