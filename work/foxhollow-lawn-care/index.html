01 / The business

Started in 2024, already well reviewed

Foxhollow Lawn Care, LLC was started in January 2024 in Charlotte, North Carolina, and is run by its owner, Christian Suggs. It does weekly or bi-weekly mowing with trimming, edging and blowing, plus leaf and fall clean-ups, pine straw and mulch, hedge trimming, overgrowth removal, and roof and gutter clean outs. It describes itself as fully insured.

It has a 5.0 rating on Google from 27 reviews, has been BBB accredited with an A rating since July 2024, and was a Nextdoor Neighborhood Fave in 2024 and 2025, with 45 faves from neighbours.

Sources: the business's Google listing, BBB profile and Nextdoor page, checked October 2026.

02 / The problem

Lawn care sites all look the same

Foxhollow has strong reviews for a business less than two years old, but no website. And most lawn care websites look alike: a green banner, a photo of a mower, a list of services. I wanted this one to feel premium, to match the business's own aim of bringing premium lawn care to Charlotte.

03 / Approach

A seasonal magazine layout

I designed the site as Foxhollow Field Notes, a seasonal magazine. The October 2026 Fall Issue leads with leaf clean-ups. Page numbers and sections such as Cover, Feature and Letters carry that idea through the page.

Palette

Forest green and a warm cream, like the cover of a good outdoors magazine, with a paper white for reading and a rust orange for small accents and the call to action.

Type

Headlines and the masthead are set in Fraunces, a soft, old-style serif that looks at home on a magazine cover, with italic words in rust. Body text and the small labels are in Inter.

Display · Fraunces

The envy of the neighborhood, all year round.

Text · Inter

Weekly or bi-weekly mowing, complete with trimming, edging and blowing.

Layout

  • Cover. A masthead with the issue date and phone number, section links set like a contents line, then a full-width photo with the cover line and the three ratings as cover badges.
  • The Feature. Page 02: a two-column article about the business, with a drop cap, a neighbour's pull quote and a numbered list of services, from "No. 1: right now" (leaf and fall clean-ups) to "No. 6: to start" (a free quote).
  • Letters. Neighbours' Nextdoor reviews set as letters to the editor, under "Notes from neighbors, and the replies".
  • Departments. The services again, grouped like the regular sections of a magazine.
  • Coverage. The service area and a free-quote form.

Interactions

Motion is kept to a minimum, as in print. On phones, a call-to-action button stays fixed to the bottom of the screen.

Mobile

The layout steps down twice. Below 1000 px the feature's columns merge, and below 720 px the masthead, cover badges and contents line stack into one column.

04 / Build

One small file, hosted on Vercel

I built this as one HTML file with CSS and a little JavaScript. There's no framework or build step. It runs on Vercel.

  • The build is small. The HTML file is about 28 KB, and it uses two font families from Google Fonts.
  • I used headings, page landmarks and labelled form fields.

Safeguards

I made this as a sample, so I added these safeguards.

  • A banner at the top and a line in the footer say it is a sample site, not affiliated with or published by the business.
  • It's hidden from search engines with a noindex meta tag.
  • The form is a demo. It sends nothing and says so.
  • Every fact comes from the business's public listings, and the page says where each one came from.
  • Photos are from Unsplash and labelled as stock in the captions and footer. They are not Foxhollow properties.
